Wet Carpet Drying: What New Bedford Homeowners Should Know

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. Up top the fibers may seem barely damp, yet the pad has filled up the way a sponge does and the subfloor under it has gotten wet as well. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. Our team in New Bedford extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

What It Looks Like

  • Paint blistering near the floor, or baseboard trim that has puffed up
  • The room smells sour and stale, especially after it has been closed for a while
  • Loose, wavy sections where the tack strip no longer holds the carpet
  • A wet spot that keeps growing even though no new water has been added
  • Socks that come away damp, or a squishing sound, when you cross the carpet
  • A floor that squeaks or feels soft under the carpet where it got wet
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Furniture Marks

Furniture legs sitting on wet carpet release wood stain and rust into the fibers. Such spots are frequently there for good, while the legs soak up water and swell.

Soaked Pad Underneath

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. It keeps feeding moisture into the carpet above and the floor below long after the surface feels dry.

Swollen Subfloor

Subfloors made of OSB or particleboard puff up and fall apart if they remain wet. Squeaks, soft spots and raised seams show up weeks after the spill is forgotten.

Water Quality Drops

Even clean water goes bad the longer it sits. It collects bacteria and dirt from the pad and fibers, which can turn a simple drying job into a removal.

Our Process

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Map the Wet Area

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry All the Layers

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reset

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Moves Outward

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

Hours 24 to 48

Smell and Mold Start

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Days 3 through 7

Carpet Starts Failing

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

After a week

Too Late to Save

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.
