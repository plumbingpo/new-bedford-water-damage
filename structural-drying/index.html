Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

New Bedford Restoration employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Daily monitoring is built into how we dry a structure, and it relies on calibrated meters, thermal cameras and thermo-hygrometers. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Why Hire a Professional for Structural Drying?

Drying a structure properly takes science, and guesswork has no place in it. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.

Signs It Is Time for Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • High humidity in affected areas despite efforts to ventilate
  • Moisture meter readings above normal in walls, floors or ceilings after water damage
  • Stained, swollen or soft spots in the drywall
  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • Wall finishes that start to peel or bubble in the days after water damage
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away
  • Shut the doors and windows of any room under drying, which keeps dehumidification working well
  • Deal with water intrusion right away, since an early start on drying gives a better result
  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
  • Do not turn off professional drying equipment to reduce noise, because every hour matters
  • Until a technician removes the equipment, keep it running day and night without breaks

Structural Drying, Step by Step

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Moisture Assessment

Using moisture meters, thermal imaging and hygrometers, we create a comprehensive moisture map of all affected areas. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline readings.

2

Setting the Equipment

Our crew sets up high-velocity air movers along with commercial LGR units in the positions the moisture map calls for. The materials being dried determine how we set the airflow and the temperature.

3

Daily Monitoring

Each day a technician visits to document progress, reposition equipment and measure moisture at every monitoring point. We keep you posted on how the timeline looks.

4

Dry Standard Verification

Final readings are documented, the equipment comes out and your property is certified at dry standard as soon as all affected materials test at acceptable levels.
