AC Leak Water Damage in New Bedford: What to Know

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. Once dust and algae block the drain, water backs up until it runs over the edge of the pan. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. An HVAC technician still has to fix the unit or unclog the line, and we plan our work around that appointment.

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ACSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. Where the wiring on the air handler is getting wet, the breaker should also go off.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Skip this step if it means crossing a soaked attic.
  4. Soak Up What You CanBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Call Both TradesYou need two calls: one to us so we can measure the wet area, one to an HVAC technician who will unclog the drain. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.
Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Read your policy, and ask us for photos and moisture readings to support the claim.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Low refrigerant or a clogged filter, either of which leads to a frozen coil.
  • A condensate drain line clogged with algae, dust and slime from the coil.
  • Water escaping from a drain pan that has cracked, rusted through or tilted.
  • The safety float switch being absent or broken, so the unit kept running.
  • Units that rely on a condensate pump, when that pump quits or gets unplugged.
If It Is Left

Where the Damage Spreads

Mold Near the Return

Mold may appear in 24 to 48 hours on damp materials by the air handler, where the return air stream can carry its spores through the ductwork to other rooms.

Insulation Around the Unit

Overflow in the attic sinks into the surrounding insulation, which keeps the drywall under it wet. Wet insulation loses its value and often has to be replaced.

Closet Walls and Platform

Closet units often sit on a wood platform over the return air box. Water rots that platform and wicks up the surrounding drywall, where it stays hidden behind the unit.

Trim and Baseboards

Trim at floor level, including the closet door frame, wicks up the leak. Fiberboard trim swells and crumbles, so it is usually replaced, not dried.

Our Process

Our AC Leak Water Damage Process

1

Measure the Spread

We use moisture meters and thermal imaging around the air handler, the ceiling or walls next to it, and the flooring outside the closet to find every wet material, not only the stained ones.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

Our extractors pull water out of the pan, the wood platform and any carpet. To let the space behind it breathe, we cut away drywall and insulation that are soaked or growing mold.

3

Dry the Structure

We monitor readings throughout the several days, usually 3 to 5, that the fans and dehumidifiers need. Access to the air handler is kept open for your HVAC technician.

4

Sanitize and Rebuild

Since condensate left standing for days is seldom clean, we apply an antimicrobial to the surfaces it touched. New flooring, baseboard, drywall and insulation follow, and fresh paint makes the area match.
