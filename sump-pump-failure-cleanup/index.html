Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in New Bedford: What to Know

The moment a basement most needs its sump pump is often the moment the pump gives out. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. The pit overflows and the basement floor starts to flood. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

Do not think of it as clean water. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. Cleanup is different as a result, with wet drywall, carpet pad and other porous materials generally removed and the rest cleaned and disinfected ahead of drying.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Keep Out of the BasementDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. The furnace, wall outlets or the cord of the pump itself could be in contact with it.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsTurn off the basement circuits at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach it from a dry spot. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Holding off until the basement empties is not the answer.
Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Sump pump losses are widely misunderstood when it comes to insurance. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Unless you bought an endorsement for sump overflow or water backup there is often no coverage, and such endorsements are capped. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.

What It Costs

What Drives the Price

  • The depth of the water and the share of the basement floor under it.
  • How long the water stood before pumping and drying could begin.
  • If water is still coming in, the cost of holding it back with a temporary pump.
  • Whether it is Category 2 or Category 3 water, since that decides what gets thrown out and disinfected.

How We Handle Sump Pump Failure Cleanup

1

Pump Out the Basement

Our crew arrives with extractors and pumps, which means your dead sump is not needed. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Our team wears protective gear to remove wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad reached by groundwater, then bags and hauls it off, leaving bare framing and concrete.

3

Clean and Sanitize

An antimicrobial product goes on after the concrete, the studs and any kept belongings have been washed. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.

The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Years of on and off cycling finally burning out an aging motor.
  • Undersized equipment that falls behind when inflow is heavy.
  • A discharge line that is blocked, crushed or disconnected outside the house.
  • Loss of power at the pump alone, from a loose cord or a tripped circuit.
  • Gravel, silt or debris in the pit jamming the impeller or clogging the intake.

What the Water Damages

Finished Basement Walls

Dirty water climbs from the slab into insulation and drywall, far higher than the line you can see. Since it is contaminated water, we remove the wet bottom portion instead of drying it.

Basement Mechanical Equipment

Sump pits tend to be located near the home's mechanical equipment. If wiring, burners or control boards got wet they may be ruined, and no appliance should be restarted until it has been checked.

Outlets and Wiring

Wall outlets near the floor are sometimes submerged in a sump flood. Before those circuits are used again, an electrician needs to inspect whatever was under water.

Boxes and Stored Items

Cardboard boxes, furniture legs, books and fabrics soak up contaminated water quickly. Anything porous that sat in it is hard to disinfect and will grow mold within days.
